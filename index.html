what I'm building

Laifu

A planning app that helps me figure out my week

I wanted more than a to-do list. Laifu has an AI in it that works the way a project manager would, so it helps me decide what my week is for. I use it every day and I'm still building it, so it changes a lot.

Here's what it does.

  • Every morning it has a plan for my day ready, and I change whatever I don't agree with.
  • Once a week it looks back at what I finished and plans the week ahead.
  • It plans around how much I actually get done.
  • It keeps track of my energy as well as my time.
  • It tells me when it disagrees with me.
  • It only makes changes on its own when they're easy to undo.

I built all of it with AI, using tea-stack.

tea-stack

pstack, reverse engineered for Claude Code

I reverse engineered pstack, Lauren Tan's agentic harness for Cursor, to understand how it works. Then I rebuilt it for Claude Code, because I prefer Opus 5.5 and was running out of it fast in Cursor. Now I have my own version to tinker with.

Here's what's in it.

  • Several AIs can attempt the same task at once. A different one judges the results, and the best parts get combined.
  • Before a change ships, Claude and Codex both try to break it. A problem only counts if they can reproduce it.
  • Every claim in a reply is marked as measured, inferred, or a guess, and a measured claim needs a file that backs it up.
  • It can teach itself how to start an app and test a feature in the running app, so it checks the real thing.
  • When the AI keeps making the same mistake, it finds the pattern and adds a check so the mistake can't happen again.
  • Rules are enforced by code where possible. For example, it can't send me a screenshot that's too small to read.

  • Codex
  • Shell hooks

how I work with AI

  • I say what I want up front

    I get much better results when I'm clear and thorough about what I want, so I work through the requirements with the AI and document as much as I can up front.

  • The AI has to show me

    AI loves to say it did something when it didn't. So I ask for proof I can look at, like logs, screenshots, or a video of the thing working.

  • It earns bigger jobs over time

    I start it on small, low-risk work and give it more as it earns my trust and build an environment of verification.

  • I stay close to writing and design

    AI still isn't good at writing, and it needs direction on design. I go back and forth with it on designs before anything gets built, and I'm involved in anything a person will read.
